# Target Fan & Problem

**Status:** DECISION + HYPOTHESIS

## Target Fan

The initial target is the passionate college sports fan whose fandom is part of a weekly routine.

This fan typically:

- Knows the team's schedule.
- Checks scores, rankings, roster news, and team updates.
- Watches games when possible.
- Consumes highlights, commentary, and analysis.
- Discusses the team with other fans.
- Cares about the team between games, not only during games.

The target is defined by behavior and intensity, not age, gender, geography, or a specific school.

Notre Dame is the first product laboratory, not the permanent market definition.

## Problem

A passionate fan's relationship with a team is fragmented across many products and channels. One place has scores, another has news, another has social conversation, another has video, and another has historical or statistical context.

The fan can get the information, but the relationship is fragmented.

## Job To Be Done

> **When I want to stay connected to my team, help me follow what matters without making me piece my fandom together across a dozen different places.**

## Product Implication

Suite should not try to replace every existing sports product. It should make the fan's team relationship easier to maintain while those products continue to coexist.

The product should prioritize:

1. Relevance
2. Convenience
3. Speed
4. Accuracy
5. Context
6. Team identity
7. Lightweight participation

## Hypothesis

**H1:** Passionate college sports fans will repeatedly use a free, team-specific digital experience if it makes following their team more convenient, relevant, and engaging than the fragmented alternatives they currently use.

## What Would Support H1

Evidence would include repeat usage, return behavior across games, meaningful content interaction, notification engagement, sharing/referrals, and requests for additional teams.

## What Would Challenge H1

The hypothesis should be reconsidered if fans try the product but do not return, if usage is limited to novelty/game-day spikes, if the experience does not solve a meaningful fragmentation problem, or if fans consistently prefer existing products for the same jobs.

## Open Questions

- Which fan moments create the strongest reason to return?
- Which information is most valuable to consolidate?
- How much community functionality is necessary before it adds value?
- Which notifications are useful rather than noisy?
- Does the value proposition hold beyond Notre Dame?
