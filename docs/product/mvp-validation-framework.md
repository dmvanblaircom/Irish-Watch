# MVP & Validation Framework

**Status:** DECISION + HYPOTHESIS

## MVP Objective

The MVP is not intended to prove the entire LND platform. It should prove that a free, team-specific experience creates enough utility and relevance for passionate fans to return.

## MVP Scope

### Core

- Home / Now
- Schedule
- Game Center
- News / Updates
- Team / Roster
- Basic team personalization
- Notifications where the underlying capability is reliable

### Lightweight Participation

Start with a small number of low-complexity interactions such as reactions, predictions, polls, or game threads. Choose based on the strongest validated fan need.

### Non-MVP

- Full social network
- Messaging
- Fantasy
- Betting
- Ticketing marketplace
- Merchandise marketplace
- Complex gamification
- Large AI assistant
- Paid consumer subscriptions
- Advertising platform

## First Hypothesis

**H1:** Passionate college sports fans will repeatedly use a free, team-specific digital experience if it makes following their team more convenient, relevant, and engaging than the fragmented alternatives they currently use.

## Validation Dimensions

### Acquisition

Questions:

- Can the product attract fans without a large paid acquisition budget?
- Which sources or sharing behaviors bring qualified fans?

Signals:

- New users
- Source of acquisition
- Team selected
- Referral/share behavior

### Activation

Questions:

- Does a new fan quickly understand the value?
- Does the fan return after the first session?

Signals:

- Team selected
- Meaningful first-session interaction
- Second session
- Notification opt-in where relevant

### Engagement

Questions:

- What makes the product worth opening repeatedly?
- Does game-day utility translate into between-game usage?

Signals:

- Sessions per active user
- Game-day usage
- Content interactions
- Game Center usage
- Notification engagement

### Retention

Questions:

- Do fans return across games?
- Does the relationship persist between games?

Signals:

- 7-day retention
- 30-day retention
- Game-to-game return rate
- Between-game return rate

### Advocacy

Questions:

- Do fans naturally share the experience?
- Do users ask for additional teams?

Signals:

- Shares
- Referrals
- Additional team requests
- Organic team expansion

### Platform Validation

Questions:

- Is Irish Watch proving a reusable product rather than a one-off site?

Signals:

- Second team launch
- Time to instantiate a team
- Configuration reuse
- Percentage of Suite behavior shared across teams
- Number of team-specific conditionals required

## Decision Gates

### Gate 1: Useful

Fans can complete important team-following jobs faster or more conveniently.

### Gate 2: Repeatable

Fans return after the initial visit and across multiple team moments.

### Gate 3: Valuable

Usage shows enough recurring value to justify continued investment.

### Gate 4: Reusable

The same product architecture works for a second team without copying the application.

### Gate 5: Monetizable Later

Only after fan value and repeat usage are demonstrated should consumer or organization monetization be expanded.

## Measurement Principle

Do not optimize for vanity metrics alone. A large first-day audience without repeat behavior does not validate the core thesis.

The goal is evidence that the product earns a durable fan relationship.
